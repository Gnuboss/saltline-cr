A first surf trip goes best when you choose a beach break with a sandy bottom, book lessons for the first days, and keep your plans flexible around the swell. Costa Rica has several places that fit that description, and the choice mostly comes down to the kind of trip you want around the surfing.

## Choose a forgiving wave

Sandy beach breaks are the easiest place to learn, because falling is more comfortable than on reef or rock. Nosara's Playa Guiones, Tamarindo, Jacó and Dominical all have beach breaks that suit beginners and surfers who are still building confidence. Reef breaks and long point waves, such as Salsa Brava and Pavones, are for experienced surfers.

## Pick the base that suits the rest of your trip

If you want the most choice of schools and places to stay, Tamarindo is the easiest base on the north Pacific coast. If you want to pair surf with yoga and wellness, Nosara is a natural choice. If you are short on time and flying into San José, Jacó is the closest option.

## Think about the season

The dry season, December to April, usually means sunnier weather and gentler waves on much of the Pacific coast. The green season, May to November, brings bigger swells and afternoon showers, so mornings matter. Beginners can surf in either season, as long as the lesson level matches the conditions on the day.

## Book lessons first, then go independent

Take a lesson on your first day or two, even if you have surfed before. An instructor will read the conditions for you and show you where to paddle out. Once you can read the line-up and handle small waves on your own, rent a board and surf independently.

## Pack for the water and the sun

A rash guard, reef-safe sunscreen and a leash you trust matter more than fancy gear. Most schools and hotels rent boards, so you rarely need to bring your own on a first trip.
